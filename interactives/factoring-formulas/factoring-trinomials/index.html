Factoring \(x^2 + bx + c\) with Algebra Tiles

Factoring a trinomial means arranging its tiles into a rectangle. The sides of the rectangle are the factors. Slide to arrange the tiles, and change \(p\) and \(q\) to build different rectangles. The picture shows \(x^2 + (p + q)x + pq = (x + p)(x + q)\).

Questions to think about

  1. Which tiles make the term \(x^2\), which make the middle term, and which make the constant term?
  2. Why is the constant term the product of \(p\) and \(q\), but the middle coefficient the sum of \(p\) and \(q\)?
  3. Look at a pile of tiles with just one \(x^2\) tile, some \(x\) tiles, and some unit tiles. How could you tell whether they can be arranged into a rectangle?
