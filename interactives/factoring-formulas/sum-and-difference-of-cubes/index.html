Factoring Sums and Differences of Cubes with SOAP

Factoring a sum or difference of cubes is about volume. Switch between the two cases, and use the slider to take the pieces apart or put them together. The signs in the factors follow the rule Same, Opposite, Always Positive (SOAP).

Questions to think about

  1. In the difference of cubes picture, how thick is each of the three pieces? Where do the three base areas show up in (a − b)(a² + ab + b²)?
  2. In the sum of cubes picture, the base of the prism is a rectangle with a square attached. Why does that make the middle term of a² − ab + b² negative?
  3. What happens to the three pieces in the first picture as the small cube gets close to the size of the big cube? What does that say about a³ − b³?
