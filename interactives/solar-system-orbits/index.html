The Eight Planets and Their Elliptical Orbits

Every planet moves on an ellipse with the sun at one focus. This page shows where the planets are on the date displayed and moves them forward in time. The orbital elements are the published values from NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory, valid for 1800 to 2050. Click a planet, or its name in the table, to see its numbers.

The period column comes from Kepler's third law, T2 = a3, with T in years and a in astronomical units. Venus has the most circular orbit (e = 0.007) and Mercury has the most eccentric (e = 0.206). The orbits are drawn flat, as seen from above the sun's north pole, and the small tilts of the orbits are ignored. The planets move counterclockwise.
