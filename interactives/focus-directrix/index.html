The Focus and Directrix Definition of the Conics

A conic section is the set of points P for which the distance to a fixed point F (the focus) is a constant multiple e of the distance to a fixed line (the directrix): PF = e · PD, where D is the point of the directrix closest to P. The number e is the eccentricity. Drag the point P along the curve, and change e to see all of the conics appear from one definition.

PF, distance to the focus PD, distance to the directrix directrix

In polar coordinates with the focus at the origin, the curve is r = ℓ / (1 + e cos θ), where ℓ is half the width of the curve measured through the focus (here ℓ = 3). The directrix is the line x = ℓ / e. When e = 0 the directrix is infinitely far away and the curve is a circle. When e is greater than 1, the curve has two branches, and the ratio PF / PD is equal to e on both of them.

Questions to think about

  1. Set e = 1 and drag P around the parabola. What is true about PF and PD at every point? Use this to explain, in your own words, why the points of a parabola are exactly the points equally far from the focus and the directrix.
  2. Move e slowly from 0.02 up to 0.99. What happens to the directrix, and what happens to the ellipse? Why does it make sense that a circle has no directrix?
  3. Pass through e = 1 and keep going. Describe what changes in the shape, and where the second branch appears. Check that PF / PD is still e on the second branch, and explain why the second branch has to exist.
  4. An ellipse has two foci, so by symmetry it should also have two directrices. Turn on the second focus and directrix. Where are they, and is the ratio of distances the same for them?
  5. For an ellipse, the readout gives the closest and farthest distances from the focus to the curve. Use them to find a and e with a = (rmin + rmax) / 2 and e = (rmax − rmin) / (rmax + rmin), and check your answer against the slider. How does this connect to the closest and farthest distances of a planet from the sun?
