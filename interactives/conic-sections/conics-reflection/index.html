Reflection Property of the Conics

Each curve is a cross-section of a mirror. Light obeys the law of reflection: the angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection, measured from the normal line to the curve. Choose a conic and watch where the reflected light goes.

Questions to think about

  1. Watch the parabola with the light pulses on. Why do all the pulses reach the focus at the same moment, even though the rays that hit the mirror far from the axis travel a different distance after reflecting than the ones near the axis? (Hint: compare the distance from a point on the parabola to the focus with its distance to the directrix.)
  2. Set the ellipse to e = 0, so it is a circle. Why do all the pulses return to the center at the same moment? Now set e = 0.6. The rays that go left and the rays that go right have very different path lengths between the two ends. Why do the pulses still arrive at F₂ together?
  3. In the parabola, make the focal length p smaller. What happens to the shape of the mirror, and what happens to how far the light travels before it reaches the focus?
  4. In the hyperbola the pulses never gather at a point. What shape do the reflected pulses form as they spread out, and why? (Hint: use PF₁ − PF₂ = 2a.)
  5. A flashlight reflector is shaped like a parabola (a paraboloid in three dimensions), not like a circle (a sphere). Use what you see here to explain why.
  6. Trace one ray and look at the angle readout. Explain why the angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection at every point of every mirror here, and why the dashed normal line is the right line to measure from.
