Sums and Differences of Distances to the Foci

An ellipse is the set of points P for which the sum of the distances to two fixed points F₁ and F₂ (the foci) is a constant, PF₁ + PF₂ = 2a. A hyperbola is the set of points for which the difference of the distances is a constant, |PF₁ − PF₂| = 2a. Drag P and watch the two distances. The bars below the picture show the two distances side by side.

PF₁ PF₂ the constant 2a

The foci are at (−c, 0) and (c, 0), with c = a · e. For an ellipse, b² = a² − c², and for a hyperbola, b² = c² − a². Turn off "Keep P on the curve" to drag P anywhere in the plane and compare the sum or difference there with the constant 2a.

Questions to think about

  1. In the ellipse view, turn off "Keep P on the curve" and drag P around. When is the sum PF₁ + PF₂ less than 2a, and when is it greater? Explain why that sum is smaller inside the ellipse and larger outside.
  2. Drag P onto the segment joining the two foci. What is the sum there, and why? Is it possible for the sum to be smaller than that anywhere in the plane? (Think about the triangle inequality.) What does this tell you about how large 2a must be compared with 2c for an ellipse to exist?
  3. A gardener drives two stakes 4 feet apart, ties a loop of string 14 feet long around them, and draws with a pencil that keeps the string taut. Explain why the pencil traces an ellipse. Find a, c, and b for this ellipse, and set the sliders to check your answer.
  4. In the ellipse view, move e toward 0, and then toward 1. What happens to the shape, and what happens to the foci? Explain each case using the idea that PF₁ + PF₂ is constant.
  5. Switch to the hyperbola. Drag P to each branch with "Keep P on the curve" on. Which distance is larger on the left branch, and which on the right? What would the difference of distances be on the vertical axis, and why is that axis not part of the hyperbola?
  6. For any point P at all, the triangle inequality gives |PF₁ − PF₂| ≤ 2c. Use this to explain why a hyperbola needs a to be less than c, which is the same as e greater than 1.
