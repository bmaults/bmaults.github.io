Slicing a Cone to Get the Conic Sections

A double cone is cut by a plane, and the curve where the plane meets the cone is a conic section. Tilt the plane and watch the curve change from a circle to an ellipse, then a parabola, then a hyperbola. Drag the picture to rotate it, and scroll or pinch to zoom.

The tilt t is the angle between the cutting plane and a plane perpendicular to the axis of the cone. If t = 0 the cut is a circle. As t increases the cut stays an ellipse until the plane becomes parallel to a line on the cone (a generator). That happens when t = 90° − α, and the cut is a parabola. Past that, the plane cuts both halves of the cone and the cut is a hyperbola with two branches. The eccentricity of the curve is e = sin t / cos α.

Questions to think about

  1. Start with the plane perpendicular to the axis and slowly increase the tilt. Describe how the curve changes. What is special about the tilt t = 90° − α, and why does the curve change type there?
  2. Why does a hyperbola have two branches? Where does each branch come from on the cone?
  3. Make the cone narrower by decreasing α. How does the tilt at which you get a parabola change? Explain this using the angle of the lines on the cone.
  4. The plane in this demo never passes through the apex. Predict what the cut looks like when the plane does pass through the apex, for a small tilt, for the tilt of a parabola, and for a large tilt.
  5. Watch the eccentricity as you increase the tilt. What value does it have for a circle? For a parabola? What does that suggest about how “stretched” each curve is?
  6. Use the face-on view. Is there any tilt where the face-on view of an ellipse is a perfect circle other than t = 0? Explain your answer.
