Paper Folding and the Conics

Take a circle drawn on a sheet of paper, with a point F marked somewhere on the sheet. Choose a point Q on the circle and fold the paper so that Q lands exactly on F. The crease is a straight line. Repeat for many points Q around the circle, and the creases outline a curve. Drag F to see which curve you get.

point Q being folded onto F highlighted crease point T where the crease touches the curve

The crease for Q is the perpendicular bisector of the segment QF, so every point on the crease is the same distance from Q as from F. The point T is where the crease meets the line from the center C to Q (in the circle view) or the vertical line through Q (in the line view). Because T is on the crease, TF = TQ. That one fact is enough to show what curve T traces.

Questions to think about

  1. Why is the crease the perpendicular bisector of QF? Explain why every point on the crease is the same distance from Q as from F.
  2. With F inside the circle, T lies on the segment from C to Q. Explain why TF + TC = R for every choice of Q. What does this say about the curve that T traces?
  3. Move F to the center of the circle. What curve do the creases outline, and what is its radius in terms of R? Explain why, using the position of T.
  4. Move F outside the circle. T is now on the line CQ but outside the segment. Explain why |TF − TC| = R, and what curve this gives.
  5. In the line view, why is TF equal to the distance from T to the line? What does this say about the curve, and where are its focus and directrix?
  6. Each crease touches the curve at exactly one point, so it is a tangent line. In the circle view with F inside, the crease bisects angle FTQ, and Q is on the extension of CT. Use this to show that the tangent line at T makes equal angles with TF and TC, and explain how this gives the reflection property of the ellipse.
