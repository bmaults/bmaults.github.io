Angle Sum and Difference Formulas: \(\sin(A \pm B)\) and \(\cos(A \pm B)\) from Right Triangles

Put \(P\) on the unit circle at angle \(A + B\). Drop a perpendicular from \(P\) to the ray at angle \(A\), meeting it at \(Q\). Then \(OQ = \cos B\) and \(QP = \sin B\), and \(QP\) is perpendicular to \(OQ\). The coordinates of \(P\) are \((\cos(A+B), \sin(A+B))\), and the two right triangles in the picture split each coordinate into two pieces.

Questions to think about

  1. What happens to the picture when \(B = 0\)? Check that both formulas still make sense.
  2. Which leg of the orange triangle changes direction when you switch from the sum to the difference, and how does that change a plus sign into a minus sign?
  3. Both formulas come from the same two triangles. Which two sides of the picture would you use to read off \(\tan(A+B)\)?
