Double-Angle and Half-Angle Formulas: \(\sin 2A\), \(\cos 2A\), and \(\sin\frac{\theta}{2}\)

Put \(X = (1, 0)\) and \(P\) on the unit circle at angle \(2A\), and join them with a chord. By symmetry, the ray at angle \(A\) is perpendicular to the chord and cuts it in half at its midpoint \(M\). So \(OM = \cos A\) and \(MP = \sin A\), which means \(M\) has coordinates \((\cos^2 A,\ \sin A\cos A)\). The midpoint formula then gives the double-angle formulas.

Questions to think about

  1. Why is the ray at angle \(A\) perpendicular to the chord \(XP\)? Use the fact that \(OX\) and \(OP\) are both radii.
  2. Move \(A\) toward \(45^{\circ}\). What is special about \(\cos 2A\), and which form of the formula shows it most clearly?
  3. Where does the half-angle formula for \(\sin^2 A\) come from in the picture? Which sign would you choose for \(\sin A\) itself, and why?
