The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, Part 2: Area Equals the Rise of an Antiderivative

The area under \(f\) between \(a\) and \(b\) equals how much an antiderivative \(F\) rises from \(a\) to \(b\). Move the ends and compare the shaded area on top with the rise on the bottom.

Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, Part 2. If \(f\) is continuous on \([a, b]\) and \(F\) is any antiderivative of \(f\) (that is, \(F' = f\)), then \[\int_a^b f(x)\,dx = F(b) - F(a).\]

Questions to think about

  1. Choose \(a\) and \(b\) so the area is negative. What does \(F\) do between \(a\) and \(b\)?
  2. If you add a constant to \(F\), which parts of the picture change and which do not?
