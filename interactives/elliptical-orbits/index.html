Elliptical Orbits and Kepler's Laws

A planet moves on an ellipse with the sun at one focus. Change the eccentricity and watch how the shape, the speed, and the swept-out area change. The semi-major axis is fixed at a = 1.

Each wedge covers the same amount of time, one twelfth of the period. Kepler's second law says the planet sweeps out equal areas in equal times, so the three wedges have the same area even though they look very different. Near the sun the planet moves fast and the wedge is short and wide. Far from the sun it moves slowly and the wedge is long and thin.
