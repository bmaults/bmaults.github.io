Algebra Seen Geometrically

Many algebra formulas are statements about area. These pages show why the formulas are true, using pictures you can change. Each page has open-ended questions at the bottom, and everything runs in the web browser.

The identities at a glance

Identities shown in these demonstrations
IdentityThe picture
(a + b)(c + d) = ac + ad + bc + bdA rectangle cut into four pieces.
(a + b)² = a² + 2ab + b²A square cut into two squares and two equal rectangles.
x² + bx + (b/2)² = (x + b/2)²A strip cut in half and glued to a square, leaving a small square missing.
a² − b² = (a − b)(a + b)An L-shape cut apart and slid into a rectangle.
x² + (p + q)x + pq = (x + p)(x + q)Algebra tiles arranged into a rectangle.
A³ ± B³ = (A ± B)(A² ∓ AB + B²)Multiplying out, with the signs remembered as SOAP: Same, Opposite, Always Positive.

1. Multiplying and squaring

  • Multiplying Binomials with an Area Model (FOIL)

    A rectangle cut into four pieces shows where the four terms of FOIL come from. It also multiplies two-digit numbers, such as 23 × 47.

  • The Square of a Binomial: Why (a + b)² = a² + 2ab + b²

    A square with side a + b is cut into four pieces, which shows why the middle term 2ab is there and why (a + b)² is not a² + b². A second view handles (a − b)².

2. Completing the square

  • Completing the Square with Rectangles

    Cut the bx strip in half, glue the halves to a square of side x, and fill in the missing corner of area (b/2)². The page then uses the result to solve a quadratic equation.

3. Factoring

  • The Difference of Squares: Why a² − b² = (a − b)(a + b)

    An L-shaped region is cut into two rectangles and slid together into a rectangle with sides a + b and a − b.

  • Factoring x² + bx + c with Algebra Tiles

    Build a rectangle from x², x, and unit tiles to see why the two numbers in the factors multiply to c and add to b. It includes factoring challenges and a table of factor pairs.

  • Factoring Sums and Differences of Cubes with SOAP

    The SOAP rule gives the signs in A³ ± B³ = (A ± B)(A² ∓ AB + B²). A step-by-step multiplication shows why the middle terms cancel, and practice problems check your factorization.
