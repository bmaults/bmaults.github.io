Completing the Square with Rectangles

To complete the square for x² + bx, think of x² as a square with side x and bx as a strip that is x tall and b wide. Cut the strip in half and glue one half to the top of the square. The picture is now a big square with one corner missing. The missing corner is a small square with side b/2, so its area is (b/2)². Move the slider to watch it happen.

The picture shows b as a positive number. For x² − bx, the same idea works with (x − b/2)², and the algebra in the last line above handles negative values of b in the same way.

Questions to think about

  1. At the start, the area of the picture is x² + bx. After the cut and glue, the area is still x² + bx, but the shape is a square with a corner missing. Why does cutting the strip in half make this work? What would go wrong if you cut the strip into thirds?
  2. What is the side length of the big square once the corner is filled in? Use the picture to explain why x² + bx + (b/2)² = (x + b/2)².
  3. Try x = 4 and b = 6. How much area did you have to add to complete the square? Check that it equals (b/2)².
  4. Completing the square adds (b/2)² to make a perfect square. Why does the number you add depend only on b and not on x?
  5. Look at the equation at the bottom. How does the number c − (b/2)² tell you whether the equation x² + bx + c = 0 has real solutions? Find a value of c where there are no real solutions and explain it.
  6. The quadratic formula is what you get when you complete the square on ax² + bx + c = 0 in general. Which step in the picture corresponds to the (b/2)² under the square root?
