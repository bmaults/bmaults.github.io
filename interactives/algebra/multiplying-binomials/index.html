Multiplying Binomials with an Area Model (FOIL)

To multiply (a + b)(c + d), draw a rectangle that is a + b wide and c + d tall and cut it into four pieces. Each piece is one of the four products in FOIL: First (ac), Outer (ad), Inner (bc), and Last (bd). The same picture explains how to multiply two-digit numbers, such as 23 × 47 = (20 + 3)(40 + 7).

Questions to think about

  1. Why does FOIL always give four terms? Use the picture to explain where each of the four products comes from, and why no product is missed.
  2. Use the picture to compute 23 × 47 by adding four partial products. Which is the biggest piece, and which is the smallest? Check the total by multiplying directly.
  3. Turn off "Draw the pieces to scale." Which products are hard to see in the scaled picture when one of the numbers is much smaller than the other? Why is it still important to include them?
  4. Describe the picture for (a + b)(c + d + e). How many pieces are there, and how many terms does the product have?
  5. If a = c and b = d, the rectangle is a square. Which pieces become equal, and how does this connect to (a + b)² = a² + 2ab + b²?
  6. FOIL is a memory aid for two binomials only. Why does the area picture still work for polynomials with three or more terms, when FOIL does not?
