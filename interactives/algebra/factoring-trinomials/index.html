Factoring x² + bx + c with Algebra Tiles

Algebra tiles make factoring a trinomial into a puzzle: arrange one x² tile, some x tiles, and some unit tiles into a rectangle. The side lengths of the rectangle are the factors. Choose p and q to build the rectangle (x + p)(x + q), and see which trinomial it makes. In the length of the x tile, x is drawn as an arbitrary length, because x can be any number.

Questions to think about

  1. Look at the rectangle for (x + 3)(x + 4). Where does each term of x² + 7x + 12 come from in the picture? Which tiles give the 7x, and which give the 12?
  2. Why must the number of unit tiles (the constant term) be the product pq, and the number of x tiles (the coefficient of x) be the sum p + q? Use the corner of unit squares and the two groups of x tiles in your answer.
  3. Start a challenge. Without moving the sliders, use the factor pairs to predict p and q. Then check. What is the strategy: which of the two numbers (the sum or the product) do you look at first?
  4. Make a trinomial where there are several factor pairs of the constant term, such as 12 or 24. How do you decide which pair is the right one?
  5. Can you build a rectangle for x² + 5x + 7 with whole-number p and q? What do the factor pairs of 7 tell you?
  6. This picture only handles positive numbers. How would the factoring x² − 5x + 6 = (x − 2)(x − 3) work with tiles? Think about what a negative tile means.
