Factoring Sums and Differences of Cubes with SOAP

Sums and differences of cubes factor with a pattern that has a memory aid called SOAP: in the factored form A³ ± B³ = (A ± B)(A² ∓ AB + B²), the signs are Same, Opposite, Always Positive. Choose a sum or a difference and the two cubes, and the page factors it. The second part shows why the pattern works, and the third part lets you practice.

Questions to think about

  1. Say the SOAP rule in your own words. For x³ − 8, which sign goes in the first factor, which sign goes between the first two terms of the second factor, and which sign goes in front of the last term? Check by multiplying.
  2. In the multiplication part, which terms cancel, and why do they cancel? What would go wrong with the pattern if the middle sign in the second factor were not the opposite one?
  3. Why is the last term of the second factor always positive? What happens to B³ when you multiply B by B²?
  4. The quadratic factor A² − AB + B² usually cannot be factored further with real numbers. Check this for x² − 3x + 9 by looking at b² − 4ac.
  5. Factor 27x³ + 8 and 64x³ − 1. For each, say what A and B are before you write the answer.
  6. The difference of squares A² − B² = (A − B)(A + B) has a different pattern. Why does a difference of cubes need a trinomial as the second factor?
