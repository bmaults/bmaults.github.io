The Square of a Binomial: Why (a + b)² = a² + 2ab + b²

The square of a sum, (a + b)², is not a² + b². The picture shows where the extra piece comes from: a square with side a + b is cut into four pieces. Switch to (a − b)² to see how subtracting works.

Questions to think about

  1. The square with side a + b has four pieces. Why are two of them exactly the same size, and where does the middle term 2ab in the formula come from?
  2. A student writes (a + b)² = a² + b². Use the picture with a = 5 and b = 3 to show what is missing. Then make b very small compared with a. Is the mistake still noticeable? Why does it matter less in that case?
  3. Use the picture to compute 103² without a calculator, by thinking of 103 as 100 + 3. Which piece is the largest, and which is almost negligible?
  4. In the (a − b)² picture, the two orange strips each have area ab, and they overlap in the red corner. Explain why the formula subtracts 2ab and then adds b² back. Check it with a = 5 and b = 3.
  5. Set b equal to a in the (a − b)² picture. What is the area of the blue square, and what does the picture look like? Does the formula give the same answer?
  6. How would the picture change for (a + b + c)²? How many pieces would there be, and what would the expanded formula look like?
